1. Emerging issues

Attacks on physical retail operations, not just data

A second wave of attacks hit supermarket refrigeration controls across Europe, with more than 100 stores affected in Belgium, Sweden, Ireland and elsewhere. The first wave hit around 700 French supermarkets in October, after which the vendor urged customers to disconnect controllers from the internet.

Impact: Internet-exposed fridge and freezer controllers were tampered with to warm up overnight and spoil stock. This is operational and supply-chain risk — lost inventory and store disruption — not only a data-breach problem.

SecureResearch daily brief, Nov. 4 · Assured weekly update, Nov. 6

More targeted, AI-assisted phishing

Phishing is becoming less “spray and pray” and more targeted. Reporting this week puts overall phishing volume up over 29% year-on-year, targeted attacks up 41%, and business impersonation up 28%, alongside increasingly realistic AI voice cloning and personas.

Impact: Help-desk, vendor-payment, and executive-impersonation fraud get harder to spot. Verification callbacks and account-reset procedures matter as much as email filtering.

Black Arrow threat briefing, Nov. 6 · F-Secure F-Alert, November 2026

MFA bypass in edge devices

CISA added a SonicWall SonicOS SSLVPN flaw (CVE-2026-19868) to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on November 6. Where SAML is enabled for SSLVPN logins, valid credentials for an alternate authentication method may bypass SAML controls — including SAML-enforced MFA. The federal remediation due date is November 20.

Impact: “We have MFA” is not enough if a VPN path can route around it. Check whether any vendor or legacy access path uses the affected setup.

2. Major breaches reported

ETS — California student records

A compromised login was used to copy files from a file-transfer site run by Educational Testing Service (ETS) between May 22 and September 18, 2026. The data included names, birth dates, home addresses, school and grade, student IDs, disability and 504 status, and test results. According to the state, it did not include Social Security numbers, bank or card numbers, or passwords. No final affected count has been published.

California student data breach: what to do

Amadeus — six Asian airlines, one vendor

Singapore Airlines disclosed about 46,000 booking records accessed via travel-technology provider Amadeus, including names, booking references, itineraries and booking class. Japan Airlines reported about 92,000 customers affected and Cathay Pacific about 53,000 bookings, with ANA, Malaysia Airlines and Asiana also reporting exposure. The airlines said their own systems were not breached.

Impact: One vendor incident became parallel disclosure duties for multiple customers at once.

Massive data breach hits six major Asian airlines

SMBC Consumer Finance — up to 320,118 borrowers

Announced November 5: hackers broke into a loan-guarantee system and sent out personal data on up to 320,118 people, including names, addresses, driving licence numbers and annual incomes. The company said it detected unauthorized access on November 2 and cut off access the same day, and that no secondary harm had been confirmed as of the announcement.

RevSpring — 1,974,069 people

RevSpring, which runs an online bill-payment platform used through healthcare providers, gave notice on November 4 for a breach reportedly affecting 1,974,069 people. Data involved may include name, home address, email, telephone number, date of birth, Social Security number, and location of service. The unauthorized access reportedly ran from May 15 to June 1 and was discovered August 17.

3. Cybersecurity laws — proposed & enacted

Health Care Cybersecurity and Resiliency Act of 2026 (S. 3315)

  • Status: Passed the Senate by unanimous consent on September 30, 2026; awaiting House action. Not yet law.
  • Major elements: Directs HHS to set minimum cybersecurity standards for healthcare entities and business associates — including multifactor authentication, encryption, and monitoring with penetration testing.
  • Breach reporting: Notices would have to state the number of people affected.
  • Timing: Requirements would take effect 36 months after enactment, with technical parameters left to HHS rulemaking.

Impact: Healthcare-specific, but a clear template — prescriptive, non-optional baselines replacing a purely risk-based approach.

Senate bill would require minimum cyber standards for healthcare · Playbook for the incoming healthcare regulatory regime

Common-Sense Cloud Security Act

  • Status: Introduced October 27, 2026.
  • Major elements: Would direct NIST to develop updated cybersecurity standards, methodologies, and recommendations for cloud-based infrastructure.

Introduction of the Common-Sense Cloud Security Act

California CCPA cybersecurity audit regulations

  • Status: In effect since January 1, 2026.
  • Major elements: Businesses whose processing presents significant risk to consumers’ security must carry out annual cybersecurity audits; first audit reports are due between April 1, 2028 and April 1, 2030, depending on revenue.

4. Regulator updates & alerts

No brand-new US SEC, FTC, or NYDFS alert was found in the last few days worth flagging as new. The live requirements below are the ones current guidance keeps pointing to.

SEC / NYDFS — disclosure clocks

  • SEC: Form 8-K Item 1.05 is due four business days after a public company determines a cybersecurity incident is material.
  • NYDFS Part 500: Notify the superintendent within 72 hours of determining a cybersecurity incident occurred. The amended regulation has been fully phased in since November 1, 2025, including expanded MFA and asset-inventory requirements.

CISA — exploited vulnerabilities & BOD 26-04

Alongside the SonicWall addition above, CISA added a WordPress Core code-execution flaw (CVE-2026-119977) to the KEV catalog on November 3, with a November 6 federal deadline. More broadly, Binding Operational Directive 26-04 replaced fixed patch deadlines with risk-based timelines; its full remediation timelines apply from December 7, 2026.

CISA warns of WordPress Core vulnerability exploited in attacks

FTC Safeguards Rule — vendor breaches

If unencrypted customer information on 500 or more consumers is acquired without authorization, a covered financial institution must notify the FTC as soon as possible and no later than 30 days after discovery. Recent dealer-industry reporting on the VinSolutions incident is a live example of a vendor breach landing on the covered businesses — not just the vendor.
