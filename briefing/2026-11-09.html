1. Emerging issues

OpenSSH “sshole” — exploited within nine hours of disclosure

A password-authentication bypass in OpenSSH, nicknamed “sshole” and tracked as CVE-2026-123158 (CVSS 8.1), was disclosed and fixed on November 5 in OpenSSH 10.6p2. It affects Linux systems using the musl C library without PAM — most notably Alpine Linux — where submitting an oversized password (257–1,024 bytes) can log an attacker into an account configured to accept no password at all. Threat-intelligence firm Previdian recorded the first exploitation attempt less than nine hours after the CVE was published and confirmed root access on an Alpine honeypot. Proof-of-concept code is public. CISA has not yet added it to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog.

Impact: Alpine is the base image for a huge share of containers, and the flaw also touches embedded and IoT devices. Patch to OpenSSH 10.6p2 — and restart sshd, because installing the update alone leaves the old daemon answering — or disable password logins. The nine-hour gap between disclosure and exploitation is the story: weekly patch windows are now too slow for this class of bug.

Help Net Security: OpenSSH flaw exploited in the wild just hours after disclosure

Agentic AI attacks are now operational

Microsoft reports threat actors using an agentic AI offensive framework, ARTEX, to scan public GitHub repositories for exposed secrets — credentials, application keys, AI inference API keys — and then pivot into cloud environments, in one case moving from a GitHub scan to exfiltration from Azure Key Vault in about five minutes. The activity is assessed as ongoing since August 2026, and the ARTEX framework itself has been found exposed on public servers. Chainalysis, Chainstack, OpenCode, Warp and WorkOS have all disclosed AI-driven attacks in recent weeks.

Impact: Secret-sprawl is now exploited at machine speed, with no human in the loop between discovery and theft. For a large enterprise this means leaked keys in code repositories and CI/CD pipelines are a live credential-theft path, not a hygiene footnote: scan repositories, rotate exposed keys, and tighten what cloud credentials can actually do.

WordPress core flaw under active exploit

CISA added CVE-2026-119977 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on November 3. It is a race condition in WordPress core’s upgrade process (CVSS 8.1) that lets an unauthenticated attacker run code on the server during the seconds-long core-update window. It affects every branch from 4.7 through 7.1 below the patched release; the fix shipped in WordPress 7.1.4 on October 28, with backports for older branches. Wordfence reported blocking more than 13,000 attacks on the flaw within a single 24-hour window.

Impact: This is core WordPress, not a plugin — any public-facing site on an affected version is in scope, including corporate blogs and marketing sites. Update to 7.1.4 or the backport for your branch, and restrict web access to wp-content/upgrade/.

2. Major breaches reported

Iris Ohyama — up to 7.7 million customers

Japanese retailer and consumer-goods maker Iris Ohyama disclosed that unauthorized access to a third-party cloud service used by its Iris Plaza online store may have exposed personal data for up to 7.7 million people — about 5.8 million registered and former members and 1.9 million non-members. Data may include names, addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, dates of birth and (encrypted) passwords. The company found a suspicious file in the external cloud service on October 28, confirmed unauthorized access on October 29, and reported the incident to Japan’s Personal Information Protection Commission and police the same day. Credit-card data was not stored in the affected service, the attacker has not been identified, and no misuse has been reported.

Impact: A retailer breach that started in someone else’s cloud. The pattern is directly relevant to any retailer whose customer data sits in vendor-operated platforms: the disclosure duty lands on you even when the system is not yours.

Neowin: Japanese retailer Iris Ohyama warns of data breach affecting 7.7 million users

Chainalysis — credentials and live tokens for all users

Blockchain analytics firm Chainalysis confirmed that an attack using what it calls “offensive agentic techniques” exposed names, usernames, password hashes and live tokens for all of its users. The company first disclosed the incident on October 30, saying then that it had no evidence credentials were touched; on November 4 it confirmed the wider exposure, after an on-path attack exposed a subset of customer passwords. It took Reactor, Data Solutions, its Know Your Transaction (KYT) interface and API, other address-screening products and its login page offline at various stages. As of its November 7 update, Reactor, KYT and Address Screening were back online with tighter controls, while Data Solutions and Chain (Beta) remained offline. Chainalysis says it has no evidence investigation data was accessed or attribution data manipulated, and has not named the attacker.

Impact: A vendor that law enforcement and banks rely on for crypto tracing lost control of its own users’ live session tokens — a reminder that a supplier’s authentication compromise becomes your incident when your teams hold accounts there. Customers were told to submit trusted IPs for allowlisting, reset passwords and rotate API keys.

DigiCert — customer data and identity documents

Certificate authority DigiCert confirmed a breach after a February 2025 server migration mistakenly exposed an internal business system to the internet; it had previously been reachable only by VPN. The system had a self-registration feature that let external users create accounts, and an authorization flaw then gave those accounts read-only access to archived customer data. One unauthorized account read customer information between June 27 and July 2, 2026. DigiCert found the accounts in June but initially judged them unable to reach customer data; a further investigation confirmed the access on October 19, and notifications began in early November. Data includes names, business email addresses, phone numbers and business addresses, and for some people driver’s licenses, passports or utility bills. DigiCert has not said how many people are affected. Its certificate authority services, CertCentral and DigiCert ONE were not affected.

Impact: A migration treated as an infrastructure adjustment rather than a security change bypassed normal review — and the exposure ran for some 16 months. Change control for network exposure is the control that failed here.

Astrana Health — social engineering led to data access

Astrana Health disclosed in its third-quarter Form 10-Q, filed November 6, that social engineering attempts during the quarter resulted in certain private or confidential information on its servers being accessed and acquired without authorization. Few details have been made public so far.

Philippines — one confirmed breach, one alleged

The Philippines’ Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) said on November 9 it is coordinating with government cybersecurity agencies after a threat actor using the name “Happy Go Lucky PH” claimed to have obtained more than 10 million taxpayer records. The claim is unconfirmed: the BIR has not confirmed a breach occurred, and the authenticity and scale of the alleged dataset have not been independently verified. Separately, the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) of the Philippines confirmed a breach affecting users of one of its platforms, discovered November 7, with exposed data reported to include names, usernames, email addresses, mobile numbers, dates of birth, addresses and Tax Identification Numbers; direct notifications to affected users went out November 9.

DreamHost — hosting credentials taken over months

DreamHost appeared in a Vermont Attorney General breach report dated November 4: an unauthorized third party exploited a vulnerability in its web-based File Manager and maintained access from about August 15 until the intrusion was discovered on October 23. Stolen data includes FTP/SFTP usernames and passwords. The number of affected customers has not been stated. At least two law firms have announced investigations into a potential class action; no filed lawsuit has been confirmed.

3. Cybersecurity laws — proposed & enacted

U.S. threat-sharing law faces a December 11 deadline

The Cybersecurity Information Sharing Act of 2015 (CISA 2015) — the law that gives companies liability protection when they share cyber threat indicators with each other and with the federal government — expires December 11, 2026. A coalition of 35 industry groups, including the U.S. Chamber of Commerce, the American Bankers Association and Airlines for America, wrote to congressional armed-services leaders on November 1 urging a 10-year extension folded into the fiscal 2027 defense authorization bill. The House draft of that bill includes the extension (through 2035) and would add AI to the topics companies may share information about; the Senate draft contains no reauthorization. Congress returns to Washington on November 9 to reconcile the two bills.

Impact: This is the legal footing for most private-sector threat sharing. If it lapses, sharing does not become illegal, but the statutory safe harbor goes away — expect legal teams to re-examine what indicators flow to government and ISACs, and watch the NDAA conference closely.

Defense One: Industry groups want cyber threat-sharing law renewed through defense authorization bill

UK Cyber Security and Resilience Bill — back to the Commons

The UK’s Cyber Security and Resilience (Network and Information Systems) Bill completed its third reading in the House of Lords on November 2 and has returned to the House of Commons for consideration of Lords amendments. It would reform the NIS Regulations 2018, bringing data centres and managed IT service providers into the regulated perimeter for the first time and creating a national-security power to direct named companies. On November 4 the government extended the bill’s carry-over deadline to April 29, 2027 — it would otherwise have lapsed on November 11. The bill has not received Royal Assent and is not yet law.

TÜV Nord: UK Cyber Security and Resilience Bill — what businesses need to know

Guatemala approves a cybersecurity framework law

Guatemala’s Congress approved a Cybersecurity Framework Law on November 3 on a national-urgency basis, with 121 votes. The law creates a National Cybersecurity Council chaired by the President and criminalizes illegal access, illegal interception, attacks on data and system integrity, computer forgery, identity theft, computer fraud and misuse of devices. It still needs sanction by the Executive Branch and publication in the official gazette; it enters into force 30 days after publication, and the Ministry of the Interior has six months from then to issue implementing regulations.

4. Regulator updates & alerts

Joint U.S. advisory on retail refrigeration controllers

CISA, the FBI, HHS, USDA and the Department of Transportation issued a joint advisory on November 6 warning that threat actors are targeting electronic temperature controllers in retail refrigeration and cold-chain monitoring systems, able to push units into continuous heating and defrost cycles. This follows the European supermarket attacks covered in yesterday’s briefing; the U.S. advisory formalizes the threat for American operators. The European Commission and ENISA had issued their own joint statement on November 4 after attacks across several EU member states.

Impact: For retailers, this is now a documented operational risk from U.S. regulators, not just a European news story — segment refrigeration controllers off the internet and off the corporate network, and treat them as OT in incident planning.

CISA adds an old Apple WebKit flaw to its exploited list

CISA added CVE-2024-27834, an Apple WebKit improper-authentication flaw, to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on November 4. Apple patched it back in May 2024 (Safari 17.5, iOS/iPadOS 17.5 and related releases); CISA’s listing confirms it is now being actively exploited against devices still running pre-May-2024 software, with a federal remediation due date of November 18, 2026. (The SonicWall SSLVPN flaw added November 6 was covered in yesterday’s briefing.)
